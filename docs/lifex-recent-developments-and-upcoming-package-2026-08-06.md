## 3. LifeX Thesis Package: AI for Science Under a Changing Federal System

### Core observation

The Trump administration's science agenda should not be described simply as “science funding up” or “science funding down.” The more useful LifeX interpretation is a change in the operating system of science:

- Congress protected much of the headline NIH budget after the administration proposed large cuts.
- Grantmaking, administrative capacity, and investigator confidence nevertheless became less predictable.
- In parallel, the federal government expanded mission-directed AI-for-science programs built around national datasets, supercomputers, foundation models, automated verification, and robotic laboratories.

### Genesis Mission

- The White House described more than fifteen agencies contributing research funding, scientific datasets, and facilities.
- The accompanying agenda included domain-specific foundation models, AI-enabled verification, autonomous laboratories, and AI-native scientific institutions.

Source: https://www.whitehouse.gov/releases/2026/07/45502/

### LifeX investment consequences

1. **Scientific talent becomes unusually available.** Researchers facing stalled grants or institutional uncertainty may move into startups.
2. **Private datasets gain strategic value.** Hard-to-recreate cohorts, instruments, and experimental histories become more important when public discovery pipelines are uneven.
3. **Execution loops matter more than isolated models.** The winning company may be the one that can repeatedly generate, verify, and improve on proprietary observations.
4. **Regulatory variance becomes an operating input.** Health companies must plan for greater policy and permission uncertainty.

### FDA peptide signal

- An FDA outside compounding panel recommended allowing BPC-157, KPV, and several other peptides despite objections from agency scientists.
- The vote was advisory, not product approval.
- LifeX framing: this is a signal that the boundary of what the system may permit is becoming less stable, with potential implications for consumer-health companies such as Ro, Hims, Gala, and adjacent platforms.

Source: https://www.npr.org/2026/07/23/nx-s1-5903202/fda-peptides-restrictions

## 4. The Non-Biobucks Business-Model Package

### Problem

Companies with rare scientific datasets often default to one “bio-bucks” partnership: a large counterparty, modest upfront economics, and contingent milestones. The strategic risk is dependence on one deep relationship before the company has built diversified recurring revenue.

### Fifteen alternatives

#### Data layer: sell controlled access

1. Annual subscription.
2. Per-cohort license.
3. Usage-based clean room.
4. Benchmark dataset.
5. Synthetic derivative license.

Comparable patterns: Tempus, Flatiron, Komodo, nference, Integral, ChartR.

#### Model layer: sell the answer

6. Inference API.
7. Target or biomarker reports.
8. Trial-enrichment score.
9. Validation service.
10. Outcome-priced decision support.

Comparable patterns: Tessel, Modella, Owkin, HealX.

#### Distribution layer: own the workflow

11. Workflow software.
12. Sponsored professional access.
13. Marketplace take rate.
14. Exclusive asset spinout.
15. Network-powered discovery platform.

Comparable patterns: OpenEvidence, Science Bridge.

### Core lesson

The higher the layer, the more effectively it protects the underlying asset and the more recurring the business can become. The partnership is not the problem; the problem is allowing one partnership to become the only business holding up the company.

### Healthcare AI shifts from pilots to operating infrastructure

- ChatGPT Health, Epic's AI scribe, Amazon Connect health capabilities, and clinical-reasoning companies such as Latent point toward AI embedded in everyday care delivery.
- Newsletter material cited SullyAI as having returned more than 30 million minutes to physicians on a lower-cost, faster inference stack.
- Recurring LifeX question: will healthcare accumulate many narrow tools, or will a coherent operating layer emerge?

### GLP-1s normalize into healthcare infrastructure

- Recent LifeX materials framed GLP-1s as moving from exceptional specialty drugs toward durable consumer and preventive-health infrastructure.
- Key themes: oral formulations, direct-to-consumer channels, cash-pay distribution, cardiovascular and renal benefits, combinations, and muscle preservation.
- Useful analogy: the transition from controversial intervention to background standard of care may resemble statins, but at greater speed.

### China becomes a structural force in drug development

- LifeX's JPM/Davos synthesis noted the increasing share of pharma licensing sourced from Chinese biotechs.
- Drivers include cost, speed to proof of concept, competitive domestic ecosystems, and Western pharma's patent-cliff pressure.
- This should be framed as structural redistribution of drug-development capability, not a temporary cycle.

### Data rights matter more as models commoditize

- As open and frontier models improve, algorithmic advantage can erode quickly.
- Longitudinal samples, paired clinical data, outcome histories, instrument networks, and exclusive rights cannot be recreated as quickly.
- Increasingly important diligence question: **“Who else can plausibly build the same dataset?”**
